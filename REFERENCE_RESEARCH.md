# Reference research

## What the references actually teach

The strongest shared pattern is not a particular color, layout, or 3D trick. It is **controlled attention**: a clear promise appears immediately, evidence is easy to scan, and motion is used to connect those two things rather than to decorate empty space. Coach Logic should borrow that discipline while keeping its own mineral-print identity.

## Reference findings

### Idolo — concise product language and restrained depth

The accessible homepage uses a compact floating utility navigation, a large two-line display lockup, generous whitespace, rounded project panels, subtle borders, soft shadows, technology chips, and direct live-project links. Its voice is first-person and outcome-led: products should be useful, fast, simple, and good to use. Motion is lightweight and staged rather than spectacle-first. [1]

**Application:** Coach Logic should open with a short positioning statement, then expose proof through real product surfaces, status labels, and direct links. Use the rounded, tactile containment principle, not Idolo's exact palette or composition.

### A1 Gallery — editorial browsing rhythm

The A1 animated portfolio reference uses a dark gallery shell, a long three-column feed, generous gutters, rounded screenshot surfaces, category filters, and concise metadata. Its navigation makes browsing feel like a structured index rather than a single-screen landing page. [2]

**Application:** Treat the work index as an editorial catalogue. Group projects by purpose, show state and role, and let each project enter with a measured reveal. Do not turn every item into a decorative card with invented metrics.

### Motion Sites Academy — cinematic proof and direct verbs

The academy reference leads with a large display headline, warm accent emphasis, atmospheric depth, featured build media, and a dense tutorial/library section. Its product language is practical: watch, learn, copy, remix, and ship. [3]

**Application:** Use a featured project scene to demonstrate one useful idea at a time. Build a short interaction loop around it: observe, understand, open the real product. Avoid copying the dark cinematic palette; Coach Logic needs a lighter, quieter canvas.

### CSS Nectar — metadata discipline and progressive disclosure

The portfolio category page keeps its chrome restrained while project cards carry the visual variety. Cards pair thumbnails with date, title, creator, category, and external-link cues. Hover reveals are lightweight and should remain understandable without hover. [4]

**Application:** Every Coach Logic project should expose what it is, why it exists, the current state, the role, the evidence link, and the technology decisions. Any reveal used on desktop must have an always-available keyboard and touch equivalent.

### Awwwards — long-form editorial sequencing

The homepage is a sequence of distinct bands: featured site, nominees, winners, academy, collections, directory, blog, and market. Large headings, small utility labels, strong gutters, framed previews, and persistent orientation controls create a professional ecosystem rather than a dense dashboard. [5]

**Application:** Build the homepage as a sequence of scenes with one dominant idea per viewport: identity, process, work, systems, experience, and closing. A compact utility header and progress signal should preserve orientation without covering content.

### Michele Du — inaccessible, so do not imitate unverified details

The live site reached a password gate. Only a sparse white access form was visible. No underlying design system, product language, motion, or case-study structure could be responsibly assessed. [6]

**Application:** Keep this reference out of the visual evidence set. Use only the general lesson that restraint can be powerful, and derive the actual system from verified references and Coach Logic's own material.

### Vellwax — inaccessible, so no visual claims

The domain did not resolve in the browser, fetch, or local DNS. No palette, composition, motion, or copy could be verified. [7]

**Application:** Do not claim the rebuild is based on Vellwax-specific details. The implementation should stand on its own evidence and remain identifiable as Coach Logic.

## Synthesis for Coach Logic

The rebuild should combine six concrete principles:

1. **Product before portfolio theatre.** Hero copy states who Coach Logic is and what he builds. Each project route exposes a truthful live or repository destination in the first viewport.
2. **One continuous spatial metaphor.** The axis rig is the spine. It moves through the hero, the Build → Break → Learn → Rebuild philosophy, the pinned work chapter, and the closing handoff.
3. **Light mineral print, not generic tech dark mode.** Warm ivory paper, charcoal ink, lichen green, lilac stone, and a controlled iron-oxide accent supply hierarchy without neon, cyan, or rainbow gradients.
4. **Motion has a reversible cause.** ScrollTrigger scrubs camera distance, orbit rotation, layer depth, and text perspective. Scrolling upward returns to the earlier state. Hover and focus effects are short, bounded, and optional.
5. **Evidence has status.** Live, delivered, evolving, repository, exploring, planned, and unfinished are explicit labels. Claims remain within the supplied project evidence.
6. **Motion never hides meaning.** Reduced-motion mode removes pinning and continuous loops while retaining static scene labels, links, and readable content.

## Recommended design language

- **Layout:** alternating editorial columns, wide pinned scenes, curved timeline lanes, and full-bleed paper bands instead of repeated centered card grids.
- **Typography:** one strong display family, one readable body family, and DM Mono only for metadata. Use fluid `clamp()` sizing and short line lengths.
- **Signature motifs:** a mineral axis mark, a translucent cursor field, and a liquid S-curve timeline.
- **Copy:** plain, first-person, outcome-led sentences. Prefer “I build tools that make complex systems legible” over abstract capability lists.
- **Interaction:** hover/focus should reveal depth, state, or a useful label. It should not be required to discover the destination.
